# Mechanism Benchmark — Transition Dynamics v2

Generated by `analysis/experiments/mechanism_benchmark.py`. Synthetic systems with known ground truth.


## 1. Estimator response by transition mechanism

Mean over 12 seeds. `A` is the v2 stiffness-alignment statistic (null = 1.00); `var ratio` and `AR(1)` are the classical critical-slowing-down signals.

| mechanism | detectable? | A | **z (vs own null)** | dG | var ratio | ΔAR(1) |
|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| **null** | NO | 0.17 | **-0.23** | 0.38 | 1.01 | +0.001 |
| **fold** | yes | 0.13 | **-0.63** | 1.22 | 2.20 | +0.046 |
| **yield** | yes | 2.93 | **+15.22** | 2.54 | 1.20 | +0.032 |
| **noise** | NO | 0.17 | **-0.22** | 0.38 | 1.01 | +0.001 |
| **rate** | NO | 0.19 | **-0.09** | 5.29 | 8.23 | +0.117 |

Empirical stationary null for A (p=6): median **0.17**, 95% band [0.11, 0.39]. Cauchy-Schwarz upper bound on the null mean: 0.29. n_eff per window ~297.

> The naive isotropic null of A = 1 is WRONG for stationary data: a stationary system's mean displacement is a sampling fluctuation shaped by Σ₀ itself, so the null sits well below 1. All decisions use z against the patient's own empirical band.


## 2. What the table shows

- **z separates the yield mechanism from stationarity**: yield +15.22 versus a stationary null of -0.23. Exogenous displacement along defended coordinates is detectable.
- **z does NOT separate the fold mechanism from stationarity**: fold -0.63 versus null -0.23. This is a real negative result and it is structural, not a tuning failure: fold escape and stationary sampling noise both live in the high-variance directions, so the alignment statistic cannot tell them apart. **A(t) is a detector of exogenous, homeostatically constrained displacement only.**
- **The classical variance signal is the COMPLEMENT, not the competitor**: variance ratio is 2.20 for the fold and 1.20 for the yield mechanism — the exact mirror of z. Each statistic detects the mechanism the other misses. This overturns the v1 framing, which treated the stiffness account and critical slowing down as rival hypotheses of which one must lose.
- **Blind to noise-induced transition, as required**: z = -0.22, dG = 0.38 against a stationary dG of 0.38. A real transition occurs and nothing anticipates it. Any estimator that appeared to predict this case would be leaking.
- **Rate-induced tipping is detected — but only by the variance channel, and trivially so** (variance ratio 8.23, dG 5.29, z -0.09). The sweep inflates variance across every coordinate, so 'detection' here is not anticipation of a specific mechanism. An earlier draft of this file asserted rate-induced tipping was detected only weakly; the numbers say otherwise and the claim is corrected here rather than removed.

## 3. Complementarity, measured

AUC for separating each transition mechanism from the stationary null, using each statistic alone. 12 seeds per cell.

| statistic | fold vs null | yield vs null | noise vs null |
|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| z (Transition Dynamics) | 0.30 | 1.00 | 0.53 |
| variance ratio (CSD) | 1.00 | 0.92 | 0.51 |
| ΔAR(1) (CSD) | 0.95 | 0.98 | 0.50 |
| dG (covariance drift) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.51 |

AUC 0.50 is chance; 1.00 is perfect separation.


### 3.1 The adversarial reading of this table

**`dG` alone scores 1.00 on both detectable mechanisms.** `dG` is the plain covariance-drift quantity that Dynamical Network Biomarker theory published in 2012. For pure DETECTION, the novel Transition Dynamics statistic adds nothing whatsoever over a fourteen-year-old method — and scores 0.30 (worse than chance) on the fold mechanism.


If detection were the claim, this benchmark would have killed it here.


### 3.2 What survives — mechanism discrimination

Detection and classification are different tasks. `dG` = 1.00 on both mechanisms means `dG` cannot tell them APART. The test below asks which statistic separates one mechanism from the other.

| statistic | fold vs yield (AUC, distance from 0.50) |
|---|:--:|
| z (Transition Dynamics) | 1.00  (|Δ| = 0.50) |
| variance ratio (CSD) | 0.00  (|Δ| = 0.50) |
| ΔAR(1) (CSD) | 0.23  (|Δ| = 0.27) |
| dG (covariance drift) | 1.00  (|Δ| = 0.50) |

`dG` also separates them here — but possibly only because the two mechanisms happen to produce different deformation MAGNITUDES in this parameterisation. Section 3.3 removes that confound.


### 3.3 Magnitude-matched discrimination — the decisive test

The yield drift amplitude is swept so that dG(yield) sweeps through dG(fold). If a statistic discriminates mechanism only because the two mechanisms differ in deformation size, its AUC collapses to chance at the matched point.

dG(fold) = **1.22** is the magnitude to match.

| yield drift | dG(yield) | dG ratio to fold | AUC dG | AUC z |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 2.20 | 2.54 | 2.09 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 1.00 | 1.28 | 1.05  ← **matched** | 0.56 | 1.00 |
| 0.55 | 0.68 | 0.56 | 0.03 | 1.00 |
| 0.42 | 0.54 | 0.44 | 0.00 | 1.00 |
| 0.35 | 0.48 | 0.39 | 0.00 | 1.00 |

`z` is normalised by ‖δ‖² and is therefore invariant to deformation magnitude; `dG` is not. At the matched point **dG collapses to chance while z holds at 1.00**, and below it dG inverts — it was tracking size, not mechanism.


**This is the only defensible novelty claim left standing.** `dG` detects; it does not classify. `z` classifies the mechanism that produced the deformation. The revised hypothesis is therefore narrowed accordingly in `docs/HYPOTHESIS.md`: Transition Dynamics is a claim about MECHANISM IDENTIFICATION, not about detection performance.


## 4. Power limit — null band width vs channel count

| channels p | 95% null band for A | band width |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 4 | [0.09, 2.84] | 2.74 |
| 6 | [0.16, 2.76] | 2.61 |
| 8 | [0.21, 2.49] | 2.27 |
| 12 | [0.30, 2.20] | 1.90 |
| 20 | [0.41, 1.91] | 1.49 |
| 40 | [0.56, 1.58] | 1.02 |

The band is wide at small p. A(t) is therefore a **cohort-level discriminator between mechanisms**, not a per-window bedside alarm, unless many channels are available. This is a property of the statistic, not a tuning problem, and it bounds what the hypothesis can claim.
